# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be straight with people and assume they are being straight with you. This
is a standards project: disagreement about the right design is the work,
not a problem to be smoothed over. Contempt for the person holding the
position is a different thing, and it is not welcome.

## What this looks like in practice

- Argue the technical case. "This breaks a plain APDS client" is useful;
  "this is amateur work" is not.
- Bring the use case. A requirement is much easier to weigh when we know
  which operator, vendor, or jurisdiction needs it and why.
- Expect to be asked for evidence, and ask for it yourself. A standard is
  a long-lived commitment.
- Accept a decision once it is made and recorded, even if you argued the
  other way. Reopen it with new evidence, not with repetition.
- Assume competing implementers are acting in good faith. Vendors with
  commercial rivalries participate here; the specification serves all of
  them or it serves none of them.

## Not acceptable

Harassment, insults, or demeaning comments; discrimination on any personal
characteristic; sustained disruption of discussion; publishing someone's
private information; unwelcome attention of a sexual nature.

## Scope

This applies in every project space: issues, pull requests, reviews,
commits, and any meeting or channel used for APX work. It also applies when
someone is representing the project in public.

## Reporting

Report conduct concerns to <rneubauer@umojo.com>. Reports are handled
confidentially, and the reporter's identity is not shared with the person
reported without the reporter's agreement.

Maintainers respond in proportion to what happened: a private word, a
public correction, temporary exclusion from a discussion, or a permanent
ban. Maintainers who fail to uphold this in good faith answer to the same
standard as anyone else.
